Optimizing Energy Consumption in Robotics Using Linear Regression for Control Parameter Prediction

Robotic systems are increasingly used across industries, but their high energy consumption raises economic and environmental concerns. To address this, we developed a linear regression-based predictive control approach that optimizes energy use by forecasting suitable control parameters. Using ROS2-Gazebo, we simulated around 1,700 pick-and-place operations on a 6-DOF KUKA KR16 L6 robot under varying payloads, trajectories, and control settings. The model successfully predicted energy-optimal parameters, reducing simulated energy consumption by 17.8%–38.7% compared to baseline controls, while maintaining task performance. These results demonstrate that even simple machine learning models like linear regression can achieve significant energy savings in robotics when integrated into real-time control environments.

Desalination of water using Freezing Desalination (Desalination Machine)

We designed and built a freezing desalination machine capable of producing 5 liters of clean water per hour from saline sources. By applying optimized thermodynamic processes and control strategies, the system achieved an 18% reduction in energy consumption compared to conventional methods. Throughout the project, I developed strong skills in thermodynamics, mechanical design, and STM32 programming, integrating hardware and software to create an efficient, sustainable water purification solution.

As part of my work with the Entrepreneur with CEI program supported by AUC and USAID, my team and I at Frozen Mind developed and experimentally investigated a novel spray freezing desalination system for sustainable seawater purification. The project aimed to optimize freshwater recovery using an indirect contact falling film freezing method, where saline water is sprayed as droplets onto cooled surfaces to form pure ice crystals while rejecting salt and impurities. The system utilizes a simple refrigeration cycle with R-134a refrigerant and ethanol as a cooling medium, enhancing heat transfer efficiency and reducing energy consumption. Our design achieved a 12% increase in water recovery efficiency compared to conventional systems and demonstrated significant potential to exceed 80% efficiency with optimized parameters. This innovative approach minimizes energy use, eliminates the need for post-washing, and offers an environmentally friendly solution to global water scarcity challenges.

Solar-Powered Intelligent Sorting and Packaging Machine for Agricultural Products

This research aims to design and implement a solar-powered intelligent sorting and packaging machine for pears. The system combines mechanical, vision-based, and robotic technologies to automate the full process of fruit handling.

The sorting process begins with a mechanical mechanism that separates pears based on size. A computer vision system, enhanced by a machine learning model with reinforcement learning, identifies the quality of pears by detecting defects and infections. Based on this classification, the machine directs fruits into appropriate packaging paths.

The packaging stage is managed by two robotic arms: one arranges large, high-quality pears into a premium layer at the top of the package, while the other organizes smaller good pears in the lower layers. This dual strategy ensures better product presentation and market value.

The system is powered by a solar energy unit, enabling operation in outdoor farm environments with reduced reliance on external power. In addition, the project integrates automation, PLCs, IoT connectivity, and ROS-based control to achieve a robust, scalable, and efficient solution.

By combining automation, robotics, AI, and renewable energy, this project provides a sustainable and cost-effective approach to improving agricultural efficiency, reducing waste, and supporting Egypt’s transition to smart farming technologies.

  • Autonomous Vision-Based Robotic System for Pear Inspection, Infection Detection, and Smart Packaging

Abstract

This research presents the design and implementation of an intelligent, autonomous sorting and packaging system for agricultural products, specifically focused on pears. Utilizing a multi-stage vision pipeline and a Jetson Nano edge-computing platform, the system integrates classical computer vision, deep learning, and geometric modeling to facilitate real-time quality control and mass prediction. The system architecture encompasses fruit localization, pathological inspection, and mass estimation via depth-to-volume mapping to drive a closed-loop robotic manipulation and conveyor control sequence.

Technical methodology

  1. Multi-Space Object Detection and Localization
    Initial fruit detection is achieved through a multi-layered classical computer vision approach. By analyzing color distributions across three distinct color spaces (HSV, LAB, and BGR), the system generates comprehensive histograms for comparison against a baseline distribution. This tiered verification ensures high-fidelity pear identification. Non-conforming objects trigger an immediate rejection protocol via a 6-axis servo-actuated gripper.
  2. Automated Pathological Inspection
    Post-localization, the pears undergo a secondary inspection phase using a pre-trained TensorFlow convolutional neural network (CNN). The model is optimized for infection detection and classification. Any fruit identified with pathological defects is systematically rejected, while healthy specimens proceed to the metric analysis stage.
  3. Volumetric Analysis and Mass Prediction
    Using an Intel RealSense D455 depth camera, the system captures a dense point cloud (exceeding 1,000 data points) to reconstruct the surface geometry of the fruit. By generating curves that define the surface plane, the system estimates the volume of the pear. These geometric parameters are then processed through an Online-Trained Linear Regression (LR) model to predict fruit mass. This mass value serves as a critical input for the closed-loop control of the robotic packaging arm, ensuring precise force and motion planning.
  4. Vision-Based Motion Control and System Synchronization
    The vision system further functions as the primary sensor for temporal tracking. By monitoring the entry and exit timestamps of fruit within the field of view, the system calculates the real-time velocity of the conveyor. This data facilitates a closed-loop feedback mechanism, synchronizing the conveyor speed with the processing latency of the vision pipeline and the robotic manipulation cycles.
